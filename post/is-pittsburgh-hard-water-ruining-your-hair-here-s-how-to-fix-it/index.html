Pittsburgh Water and Hair: Check Hardness and Filter Claims

Craft Collective Team · 2026-01-18

Reviewed November 22, 2026

Start with the water record, not a diagnosis from how hair feels

A change in how your hair looks or feels is worth discussing, but it is not a measurement of water hardness. Keep the observation separate from the explanation you are considering. This guide helps organize water information and a salon conversation; it does not diagnose a hair or scalp condition or prescribe a cleansing schedule.

What hardness means

The U.S. Geological Survey describes hardness mainly in terms of dissolved calcium and magnesium. Record the reported value, its unit and the date rather than using a city name as the measurement. A result expressed as calcium carbonate in mg/L should not be compared with a number in another unit without establishing the conversion.

Identify the supplier for the actual property, including whether there is any building-level treatment. Pittsburgh Water's FAQ is a starting point for its customers. A supplier's report and your own household record should retain their source and scope; this article does not assign one hardness value to every Pittsburgh-area address.

A shower-filter claim is not automatically a softening claim

Ask what an exact device model is designed and independently certified to reduce. NSF describes NSF/ANSI 177 shower-filter certification in terms of free available chlorine reduction. That certification alone does not establish removal of the calcium and magnesium that define hardness.

NSF/ANSI 44 concerns residential cation-exchange water softeners that reduce hardness. These are different performance questions. Do not treat a product's use of the words filtering, purifying or softening as a substitute for its specific certified claim.

Record the model, listing or test reference, intended use conditions, replacement requirements and the unresolved question. A product recommendation should address the actual objective and the installation context. This guide does not recommend a brand, promise a hair result or supply installation instructions.

Bring observations and service history to the stylist

Write down what changed, when you first noticed it, the products and tools you used, and any recent color or other service. Bring the relevant labels or product instructions rather than relying on a broad term such as detox. Ask which part of the proposed salon plan addresses the concern, what the appointment includes and what home instructions apply to your own service.

A one-time change in washing water or product is an observation, not proof of the cause. Avoid converting a feeling of dryness, a change in lather or a photograph into a confirmed mineral reading. The useful record keeps what happened, what is known about the water, and what remains uncertain in separate fields.

There is no universal clarifying or chelating timetable in this guide. Ask your stylist about the proposed plan in the context of your actual hair and service history, and follow the applicable product instructions. A water-treatment claim and a cosmetic-service plan need their own evidence.

Use a short evidence record before buying or booking

Download the blank water-and-salon evidence record (CSV). It has separate rows for the water source, an exact device claim and the salon discussion. All three start unresolved; no local hardness result, certified device or treatment outcome has been filled in for you.

For example, an invented consultation note might say that the hair feels different after a move and that a shower device advertises chlorine reduction. That note identifies two questions: what the new supplier's water information says, and whether the device's certified claim addresses the intended objective. It does not establish that hardness caused the change or that the device softens the water.

Plan the consultation

Review the service options, choose the appropriate studio, and use the booking information to discuss a service-specific plan. Keep the water record and the stylist's instructions together so the next conversation starts from the same information.
