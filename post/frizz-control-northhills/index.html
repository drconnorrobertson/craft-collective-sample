Why Your Hair Gets Frizzy and How to Control It

Craft Collective Team · 2026-03-09

Frizz is one of the most common concerns among clients at Craft Collective Salon Group in the North Hills and greater Pittsburgh area. Between humidity, seasonal shifts, hard water, and everyday styling habits, frizz can show up even when your hair is otherwise healthy. Understanding what causes frizz and how to manage it helps you create smoother, more predictable hair with less effort. This guide explores the science behind frizz, the environmental factors that make it worse, and the strategies that actually help. For long term planning and a deeper approach to hair structure, clients can explore the North Hills hair salon guide for additional support.


What Frizz Actually Is

Frizz occurs when the hair shaft loses moisture balance and the cuticle lifts, allowing external humidity to penetrate the strand. This creates swelling, roughness, and unpredictable texture.


Frizz is caused by:

  • Lifted cuticles

  • Uneven moisture inside the hair

  • Environmental humidity

  • Mechanical damage

  • Chemical damage

  • Surface dryness


Once the cuticle is disrupted, frizz becomes difficult to control without targeted care.


Why Pittsburgh’s Climate Makes Frizz Worse

The North Hills and Pittsburgh region experiences large humidity swings throughout the year.


Factors include:

  • High humidity in summer

  • Dry forced air in winter

  • Constant temperature fluctuations

  • Frequent rain and moisture in spring and fall


These environmental changes make frizz a year round concern.


How Hard Water Contributes to Frizz

Minerals in hard water coat the hair, making it rough and dry.


Hard water increases frizz by:

  • Disrupting the cuticle

  • Reducing softness

  • Blocking moisture absorption

  • Making conditioners less effective


Chelating treatments help restore smoothness.


How Hair Texture Affects Frizz Levels

Some textures naturally frizz more than others due to their structure.


Straight hair

Frizz shows up as flyaways and static


Wavy hair

Frizz disrupts pattern and creates puffiness


Curly hair

Frizz expands the curl and reduces definition


Coily hair

Frizz creates tangling and shrinkage

Knowing your texture helps determine the best frizz control strategy.


The Role of Porosity in Frizz

Porosity determines how easily moisture enters and exits the hair.


High porosity

Absorbs moisture quicklyLoses moisture quicklyHighly frizz prone


Medium porosity

Balanced moisture behaviorFrizzes when damaged or dehydrated


Low porosity

Resists moisture absorptionFrizzes from surface dryness

Products and techniques should be tailored to your porosity.


How Heat Styling Impacts Frizz

Excessive heat lifts the cuticle, making hair more frizz prone.


Heat causes:

Dryness

Loss of elasticity

Increased static

Weakened cuticle layers


Using heat protectant and lower temperatures prevents long term frizz cycles.


How to Reduce Frizz in the Shower

Frizz control begins during cleansing and conditioning.


Tips:

  • Use hydrating shampoos

  • Apply conditioner generously

  • Detangle with a wide tooth comb

  • Rinse with cool water to seal cuticles

  • Avoid aggressive towel drying


Water temperature and technique matter as much as products.


Weekly Treatments for Frizz Control

Targeted treatments help create smoother, healthier hair.


Effective treatments include:

  • Deep hydration masks

  • Bond repair

  • Glossing or glazing

  • Protein balancing treatments

  • Steam hydration


A weekly treatment supports long term smoothness.


Best Styling Products for Frizz Management

The right product lineup seals the cuticle and protects hair from moisture changes.

Helpful options:

  • Anti humidity sprays

  • Lightweight creams

  • Serums for mid length and ends

  • Foams for texture control

  • Leave ins with smoothing benefits


Avoid products that build up or create stiffness.


Air Drying vs Blow Drying for Frizz

Both methods can work if executed properly.


Air drying:

  • Ideal for curls and waves

  • Reduces heat related frizz

  • Requires moisture based products


Blow drying:

  • Creates smoother results

  • Works well for straight or wavy styles

  • Requires heat protectant and controlled airflow


Technique determines outcome more than method.


Protective Styles for Frizz Prevention

Strategic hairstyles minimize exposure to humidity and friction.


Try:

  • Loose braids

  • Soft buns

  • Low ponytails

  • Claw clip styles

  • Pin backs with minimal tension


These styles reduce swelling and cuticle disruption.


Nighttime Habits to Reduce Frizz

Friction while sleeping can disrupt the cuticle and create frizz.


Improve nighttime results by:

  • Sleeping on a silk pillowcase

  • Using a loose silk scrunchie

  • Applying a small amount of leave in before bed

  • Avoiding tight styles overnight


Nighttime routine matters as much as daytime styling.


Weather Proofing Your Hair

Humidity control strategies help hair resist environmental changes.


To weather proof hair:

  • Apply anti humidity sprays last

  • Use light serums to seal the cuticle

  • Avoid heavy oils that mix poorly with moisture

  • Refresh with lightweight mists


Weather proofing makes hair more predictable outdoors.

When You Should Gloss for Frizz

Glossing smooths the cuticle, boosts shine, and improves frizz control.

Ideal gloss timing:

Every 6 to 8 weeksAfter a season changeFollowing a clarifying or chelating treatment

Gloss is one of the most effective frizz reducers.

Building a Long Term Frizz Control Routine

Consistency is key to keeping frizz manageable in varying conditions.


A balanced routine includes:

  • Hydration

  • Porosity specific treatments

  • Weather proofing products

  • Heat protection

  • Protective styles

  • Seasonal adjustments

Stylists personalize frizz plans based on texture and lifestyle.


Frizz is a product of texture, porosity, environment, and hair health. For clients at Craft Collective Salon Group in the North Hills and Pittsburgh, humidity, hard water, and seasonal weather patterns make frizz control especially important. With the right hydration, treatments, styling techniques, and protective habits, you can maintain smoother, more manageable hair year round.
